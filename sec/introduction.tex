\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}


Foundation models for 3D data now dominate large-scale shape analysis. Transformers pretrained on unlabeled point clouds learn semantic and geometric structure and reach strong performance in classification, segmentation, and retrieval. However, little is known about whether these representations encode the underlying topology of shapes: their connected components, loops, or voids.

Most evaluations measure geometric fidelity or semantic transfer, not structural consistency. We therefore ask: \textit{Do 3D encoders learn topology?} If their embeddings capture persistent topological features, they may generalize across geometric domains more reliably. Yet current pretraining, often borrowed from text or image tasks, ignores geometric invariances, producing encoders that generalize poorly beyond the training distribution.

\subsection{Current State of Research}

Early self-supervised 3D encoders such as Point-BERT (CVPR2022)~\cite{pbert}  and Point-MAE (ECCV2022)~\cite{pmae} demonstrated that transformers can model local and global geometric cues in point clouds. They rely on masked modeling or token reconstruction to learn shape embeddings that capture semantic and geometric structure. However, these works evaluate only geometric or semantic accuracy, leaving their ability to represent topology unexplored.

Topology studies how a shape is connected, independent of metric details. It describes invariants, connected components, tunnels, voids, that remain unchanged under continuous deformation. Determining whether 3D encoders represent such invariants reveals if they capture intrinsic structure rather than surface geometry.

Topological Data Analysis (TDA) quantifies these invariants through persistent homology, which tracks how features appear and disappear as a filtration parameter varies. The resulting persistence diagrams summarize structure across scales and are stable under perturbations~\cite{cohen2007stability}. Despite wide use in physics and biology, they are expensive to compute on large 3D samples.

Recent work bridges TDA and deep learning~\cite{optimizing_persistent_homology, persistent_homology_seg, } by embedding persistence information into neural networks~\cite{neural_approximation_graph_topological_features} or using topological descriptors as regularizers~\cite{topological_autoencoders,top_layers,topology_activations,top_reg}. Yet it remains unknown whether modern 3D transformers already encode topological cues implicitly. This unresolved question motivates our study.

\subsection{Motivations}

Our study follows three motivations.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Interpretability.} Determining whether 3D encoders preserve topological features clarifies what these models actually learn.
    \item \textbf{Benchmarking.} Existing datasets confound geometry and topology; a controlled benchmark is needed to isolate structural learning.
    \item \textbf{Bridging TDA and representation learning.} If pretrained embeddings correlate with persistent homology, this would suggest a path toward topology-aware foundation models. Each motivation corresponds respectively to the analyses in Sections \ref{sec:topogen}, \ref{ssec:model_probing} and \ref{ssec:changing_pretraining_data_distribution}
\end{enumerate}

We therefore ask:

\begin{center}
    \fbox{\parbox{0.9\linewidth}{
    \textit{To what extent do current 3D shape encoders capture the topological structure of 3D shapes, and can their learned representations be leveraged as scalable proxies for topological computations?}
    }}
\end{center}

\subsection{Contributions}

The contributions of this report are three-fold:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A topology-controlled dataset.} We introduce DONUT (Section \ref{sec:topogen}), a synthetic dataset of 3D shapes with controlled topology (number of components $\beta_0$ and genus $g$), built from families of parametric shapes.
    \item \textbf{A quantitative analysis of structural awareness.}We propose quantitative analyses linking encoder embeddings to persistent-homology descriptors using linear probing and Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA). (Sections \ref{sec:used_models} and \ref{sec:experiments}).
    \item \textbf{Architectural and training insights.} We benchmark leading 3D transformers (Point-BERT, Point-MAE, Point-M2AE, PCP-MAE) and show that they encode geometry but not topology. Together, these results define a first controlled testbed for topology-aware 3D representation learning.
\end{itemize}
